Based on the documents provided, there is no mention of fluid drag or water surface effects. However, based on fluid dynamics, **yes**, being on the surface of the water does affect the drag, and the coefficient is generally **less** than if the robot were fully submerged.

Here is why:
*   **Reduced Contact Area:** When floating on the surface, the microrobot is only partially submerged. The viscous drag only acts on the portion of the robot that is in the water. 
*   **Negligible Air Drag:** The part of the robot exposed to the air experiences almost zero drag because the viscosity of air is drastically lower than that of water.
*   **Stokes Drag:** For a fully submerged spherical microrobot at very low speeds (low Reynolds number), drag is calculated using Stokes' law ($F_d = -6\pi\eta R v$). If the robot is floating exactly halfway in the water, the effective viscous drag coefficient would be roughly half of the fully submerged value.

At the micro-scale, surface tension dictates exactly how deep the robot sits in the water, which determines the exact reduction in the drag coefficient.
