Excellent question. This is a great example of how the same physical formula can be written in two different mathematical notations: one using standard vector algebra and the other using matrix-vector notation (which is often more convenient for computation and more advanced physics).

Let's show how the two formulas are perfectly equivalent.

### The Two Formulas

**Formula 1 (from your image, using matrix notation):**
$$
\mathbf{b}(\mathbf{r}, \mathbf{m}) = \frac{\mu_0}{4\pi \|\mathbf{r}\|^3} \left( 3\hat{\mathbf{r}}\hat{\mathbf{r}}^T - \mathbb{I}_3 \right) \mathbf{m}
$$

**Formula 2 (from the report, using vector notation):**
$$
\mathbf{B}(\mathbf{r}) = \frac{\mu_0}{4\pi} \left( \frac{3\mathbf{r}(\mathbf{m} \cdot \mathbf{r})}{|\mathbf{r}|^5} - \frac{\mathbf{m}}{|\mathbf{r}|^3} \right)
$$

The goal is to prove that Formula 1 simplifies to Formula 2.

---

### Step-by-Step Derivation

Let's start with Formula 1 and transform it.

**Step 1: Distribute the magnetic moment vector `m`**

The term $(3\hat{\mathbf{r}}\hat{\mathbf{r}}^T - \mathbb{I}_3)$ is a 3x3 matrix. We can distribute the vector $\mathbf{m}$ to each term inside the parenthesis:
$$
\mathbf{b} = \frac{\mu_0}{4\pi \|\mathbf{r}\|^3} \left( (3\hat{\mathbf{r}}\hat{\mathbf{r}}^T)\mathbf{m} - (\mathbb{I}_3)\mathbf{m} \right)
$$
We know that multiplying any vector by the identity matrix $\mathbb{I}_3$ gives back the same vector, so $\mathbb{I}_3\mathbf{m} = \mathbf{m}$.
$$
\mathbf{b} = \frac{\mu_0}{4\pi \|\mathbf{r}\|^3} \left( 3(\hat{\mathbf{r}}\hat{\mathbf{r}}^T)\mathbf{m} - \mathbf{m} \right)
$$

**Step 2: Understand the Outer Product term $\hat{\mathbf{r}}\hat{\mathbf{r}}^T \mathbf{m}$**

This is the most important step.
*   $\hat{\mathbf{r}}$ is the unit vector in the direction of $\mathbf{r}$. It's a column vector (3x1).
*   $\hat{\mathbf{r}}^T$ is the transpose, making it a row vector (1x3).
*   $\hat{\mathbf{r}}\hat{\mathbf{r}}^T$ is the **outer product**, which results in a 3x3 matrix.

Matrix multiplication is associative, which means $(\hat{\mathbf{r}}\hat{\mathbf{r}}^T)\mathbf{m} = \hat{\mathbf{r}}(\hat{\mathbf{r}}^T\mathbf{m})$. Let's look at the term in the parenthesis:
*   $\hat{\mathbf{r}}^T \mathbf{m}$ is the product of a row vector (1x3) and a column vector (3x1). This results in a 1x1 matrix, which is just a scalar.
*   This scalar is exactly the definition of the **dot product**: $\hat{\mathbf{r}}^T \mathbf{m} = \hat{\mathbf{r}} \cdot \mathbf{m}$.

Therefore, we have the crucial identity:
$$
(\hat{\mathbf{r}}\hat{\mathbf{r}}^T)\mathbf{m} = \hat{\mathbf{r}}(\hat{\mathbf{r}} \cdot \mathbf{m})
$$
The right side means: calculate the scalar dot product $(\hat{\mathbf{r}} \cdot \mathbf{m})$, and then scale the vector $\hat{\mathbf{r}}$ by that amount.

**Step 3: Substitute the identity back into the formula**

Replacing $(\hat{\mathbf{r}}\hat{\mathbf{r}}^T)\mathbf{m}$ with our new expression, we get:
$$
\mathbf{b} = \frac{\mu_0}{4\pi \|\mathbf{r}\|^3} \left( 3\hat{\mathbf{r}}(\hat{\mathbf{r}} \cdot \mathbf{m}) - \mathbf{m} \right)
$$

**Step 4: Replace the unit vector $\hat{\mathbf{r}}$ with its definition**

Remember that the unit vector is defined as $\hat{\mathbf{r}} = \frac{\mathbf{r}}{\|\mathbf{r}\|}$. Let's substitute this everywhere.
$$
\mathbf{b} = \frac{\mu_0}{4\pi \|\mathbf{r}\|^3} \left( 3 \left( \frac{\mathbf{r}}{\|\mathbf{r}\|} \right) \left( \frac{\mathbf{r}}{\|\mathbf{r}\|} \cdot \mathbf{m} \right) - \mathbf{m} \right)
$$
We can pull the scalar $1/\|\mathbf{r}\|$ out of the dot product:
$$
\mathbf{b} = \frac{\mu_0}{4\pi \|\mathbf{r}\|^3} \left( 3 \left( \frac{\mathbf{r}}{\|\mathbf{r}\|} \right) \frac{(\mathbf{r} \cdot \mathbf{m})}{\|\mathbf{r}\|} - \mathbf{m} \right)
$$
Combine the terms in the first part:
$$
\mathbf{b} = \frac{\mu_0}{4\pi \|\mathbf{r}\|^3} \left( \frac{3\mathbf{r}(\mathbf{r} \cdot \mathbf{m})}{\|\mathbf{r}\|^2} - \mathbf{m} \right)
$$

**Step 5: Distribute the outer coefficient**

Finally, let's multiply the outer term $\frac{\mu_0}{4\pi \|\mathbf{r}\|^3}$ into the parenthesis:
$$
\mathbf{b} = \frac{\mu_0}{4\pi} \left( \frac{3\mathbf{r}(\mathbf{r} \cdot \mathbf{m})}{\|\mathbf{r}\|^3 \|\mathbf{r}\|^2} - \frac{\mathbf{m}}{\|\mathbf{r}\|^3} \right)
$$
Combining the denominators in the first term ($\|\mathbf{r}\|^3 \|\mathbf{r}\|^2 = \|\mathbf{r}\|^5$):
$$
\mathbf{b} = \frac{\mu_0}{4\pi} \left( \frac{3\mathbf{r}(\mathbf{r} \cdot \mathbf{m})}{\|\mathbf{r}\|^5} - \frac{\mathbf{m}}{\|\mathbf{r}\|^3} \right)
$$

This is exactly Formula 2.
